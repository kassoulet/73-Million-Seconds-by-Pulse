volumesprites — a cloud of additive sprites from a 3D field

The final scene (about 245–262 s) does not draw polygons at all (renderoff disables the rasteriser, only the camera and transform set-up still run). volumesprites cudrkgh {} {SCREEN} {kubes box01 OurVolume} walks the 16×16×16 field that metaballs ... test2.mbs fills every frame; for each voxel with 35·value ≥ 1 (clamped to 120) it takes the voxel's position inside the bounding box of box01, transforms it with the camera of the scene (last object matrix × camera, kept in globals at 0x1286d0) and projects it with the usual x·f/z. The sprite is a square of 35·value·150/z pixels (skipped when smaller than 3.5), so dense parts of the blobs give big, near ones even bigger. The texture and palette do not come from the scene: the run function reads the global “current material” [0x10d91c] (texture struct at +0x54: data at +0x14, width/height as floats at +0x18/+0x1c; palette at +0x68), which the flare effects leave pointing at the last light material. Measured in the original at t = 237 s (hook on the blitter call at 0x192f8): the material is f2.omni01, the texture is flare2 (128×128 stored with a stride of 256, exactly what flares uses; the sprite blitter takes 128.0 as the texture size so one sprite maps these 128 texels) and the colour is (0.38, 0.553, 0.494), a teal tint, hence star-like sprites. The palette is a 256-entry ramp pal[i] = i/160 · colour · 256 (0x21b9b) whose bytes are kept even (pal[160] = 0x608e7e in the original).

The blitter 0x58f04 is hand-written asm with self-modifying code: the texture and palette addresses and the per-pixel texture steps are patched into the instruction stream. Edges are converted to 16.16 fixed point with the classic “add 1.5·236” trick (the low mantissa dword is then the rounded integer), clipped, and u/v are walked as 8.16 values whose integer byte lives in cl/ch (so the texture index is simply ecx and wrapping at 256 is free). The additive blend is a SWAR saturating add: since both operands have even bytes, bits 8, 16 and 24 of the 32-bit sum are the carries, and carry − (carry>>8) turns them into 0xff masks.

Per-pixel loop (original 0x5910a)

5910a  mov  ebx, dword [edi]               ; frame buffer pixel
5910c  mov  al, byte [ecx + 0x1869f]       ; texel = tex[v*256+u]   (0x1869f patched with the texture address, ecx = v:u)
59112  and  ebx, 0xfefefefe                ; drop the low bit of every channel
59119  mov  eax, dword [eax*4 + 0x1869f]   ; palette entry (second patched address)
59120  add  esi, 0xbc614e                  ; u fraction += du (immediate patched per sprite) ...
59126  adc  cl, 0xc                        ; ... u integer += du integer + carry (immediate patched)
59129  add  eax, ebx                       ; add, channels may overflow into bits 8/16/24
5912b  mov  edx, eax
5912d  add  edi, 4
59130  and  edx, 0x1010100                 ; the three carry bits
59136  mov  ebx, edx
59138  shr  edx, 8
5913b  sub  ebx, edx                       ; 0x100-1, 0x10000-0x100, 0x1000000-0x10000 = 0xff masks
5913d  or   ebx, eax                       ; saturate
5913f  dec  ebp                            ; width counter
59140  mov  eax, 0
59145  mov  dword [edi - 4], ebx
59148  jne  0x5910a
